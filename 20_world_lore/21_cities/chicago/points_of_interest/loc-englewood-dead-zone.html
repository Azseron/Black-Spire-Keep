Englewood — What Went Wrong

Englewood wasn’t destroyed by a single catastrophe.
It was bled out by a bad decision, made quietly, and covered up efficiently.

The Short Version

Englewood was the site of the first large-scale containment failure in Chicago—and the place where the government learned that Gates can’t be handled like riots or disasters.

Everything that followed across the city was shaped by what happened here.

The Incident (Late Winter → Early Spring 2025)

A Gate opened beneath abandoned rail infrastructure near the old industrial corridors.
At first, it looked like another low-tier urban Gate:

  • Small monster incursions
  • Limited mana bleed
  • Containable footprint

Federal response classified it as “manageable.”

That was wrong.

The Critical Failure

1. The Gate Was Left Partially Active

Instead of a full clear, containment teams:

  • sealed surface access
  • deployed sensors
  • delayed entry

Why?

  • manpower shortages
  • inter-agency conflict
  • fear of civilian exposure
  • pressure to “not escalate”

This allowed mana saturation to build.

2. Englewood’s Infrastructure Made It Worse

Englewood had:

  • abandoned basements
  • old utility tunnels
  • undocumented storm drains
  • vacant housing stock

Mana didn’t stay put.
It spread sideways, seeping into places no map fully captured.

3. Monsters Didn’t Just Emerge — They Nested

Creatures:

  • moved into empty buildings
  • used basements as lairs
  • learned patrol patterns
  • avoided daylight

This wasn’t a wave.
It was urban entrenchment.

The Black Week

For roughly six days, Englewood experienced:

  • unexplained fires
  • power failures
  • “gang violence” spikes
  • missing persons
  • first responder withdrawals

Police scanners recorded things they weren’t trained to describe.

Then… silence.

The Response Nobody Talks About

Federal authorities:

  • evacuated several blocks quietly
  • reclassified the zone as structurally unsafe
  • erected “temporary” barriers
  • restricted utility access

Then they did something new.

They let hunters in.

Not officially.
Not publicly.
But deliberately.

Englewood became the first proving ground for freelance containment.

The Cleanup (And the Lie)

The Gate was eventually neutralized, but:

  • not all monsters were killed
  • some escaped underground
  • some adapted to surface conditions
  • mana residue never fully dissipated

Official story:

“Infrastructure failure combined with criminal activity.”

Unofficial truth:

Englewood was a lesson written in bodies.

What Englewood Is Now (June 2025)

  • Sections are walled off
  • Others are “open” but watched
  • Utilities are unreliable by design
  • Surveillance is heavy but indirect
  • The ground feels wrong in places

Hunters know:

  • it’s not clean
  • it’s not safe
  • it’s not finished

The government knows:

  • full disclosure here would unravel everything

Why Englewood Matters to the Campaign

Englewood is:

  • proof Gates can metastasize
  • the reason Chicago’s response is so harsh now
  • a source of feral monsters
  • a cult recruitment ground
  • a place where people saw too much

It’s why:

  • Lakeshore is locked down fast
  • the Loop is watched obsessively
  • the Subterranean Market expanded
  • hunters are tolerated instead of crushed

Rumors Hunters Trade About Englewood

  • “Something’s still alive down there.”
  • “They never found the original anchor.”
  • “A crew went in last month. No exit log.”
  • “Cores from Englewood behave wrong.”
  • “That place remembers violence.”

Some of those are true.

Tone Lock

Englewood isn’t a dungeon.
It’s a scar.

You don’t clear it.
You survive it.
And you don’t forget what it taught the city.
