GM Guidelines for Running the Criminal Core Economy

These guidelines govern how the Chicago criminal underworld behaves in play.

The goal is tension, not punishment.
The goal is pressure, not shutdown.

1. Do Not Punish First Offenses Immediately

Hunters skimming small amounts is common.

First-time illegal sales should:

  • Succeed
  • Feel dangerous
  • Leave lingering uncertainty

Immediate catastrophic consequences discourage interesting choices.

2. Escalate Consequences Gradually

Illegal behavior should trigger:

1st instance → Clean sale
2nd instance → Broker curiosity
3rd instance → Territorial friction
4th instance → Surveillance / pressure
5th instance → Enforcement or leverage

Escalation should feel organic.

3. Criminal Consequences Are Personal, Not Legal

The underworld does not:

  • Call police
  • File reports
  • Issue warnings

They:

  • Cut access
  • Demand repayment
  • Enforce territory
  • Apply pressure quietly

DPS consequences are bureaucratic.
Criminal consequences are immediate and personal.

4. Reputation Spreads Slowly

Underworld Standing should move between -3 to +3.

Reputation changes:

  • After major deals
  • After betrayals
  • After territory violations
  • After exclusivity agreements

Do not swing reputation wildly without clear cause.

5. A-Rank Cores Are Political Events

A-core transactions:

  • Always involve Tier 3 buyers
  • Always create memory in the network
  • May create future leverage

They should never feel routine.

6. Money Is Power, But Leverage Is Stronger

Cash is temporary.

Favors, exclusivity agreements, and obligations create:

  • Long-term hooks
  • Story momentum
  • Moral tension

Encourage brokers to offer:
"I can do better, if you owe me."

7. Keep the Underworld Competent, Not Omniscient

Brokers are:

  • Smart
  • Connected
  • Experienced

They are not:

  • Psychic
  • All-seeing
  • Automatically aware of every lie

Use Reaction Rolls and Influence Contests.
Do not pre-decide outcomes.

8. Social Encounters Should Feel Tactical

Treat negotiations like combat:

  • Stakes defined
  • Skills matter
  • Margin of success matters
  • Long-term consequences tracked

This is Social Engineering in action.

9. Let Players Define Their Moral Line

Do not force corruption.
Do not force compliance.

Present:

  • Temptation
  • Risk
  • Opportunity

Let them choose.

The world reacts accordingly.

10. Always Leave Room for Retreat

Even if they overstep:

  • Give warning signs
  • Give negotiation options
  • Give social escape routes

Campaign tension thrives on choices, not traps.
