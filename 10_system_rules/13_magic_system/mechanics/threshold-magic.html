Threshold Magic

  1. The Core Mechanic
  • No FP Costs: Spells do not cost Fatigue Points (FP) to cast. Instead, they accumulate Threshold (T).
  • Safe Threshold: You can cast safely as long as your accumulated T stays below your campaign's defined Safe Threshold limit.
  • Slow Recovery: Threshold does not reset after every fight. It recovers slowly over time at a fixed rate (e.g., hours or days), making magic an attrition resource during Gate raids.
  1. Base Spell Costs

Whenever a mage casts a spell, it generates a base amount of Threshold based on its narrative and mechanical weight:

  1. The Magery Soft Cap (Overcasting)

This is your primary governor on magic scaling:

  • You cannot spend more Margin of Success (MoS) on a single effect category (Damage, Area, Duration, etc.) than your Magery level.
  • The Penalty: You are allowed to exceed this limit, but for every point of MoS spent beyond your Magery cap in a specific category, the Threshold cost for that category doubles exponentially (e.g., 1 over = ×2, 2 over = ×4, 3 over = ×8).
  1. Gate Environment Penalties (Mana Pressure)

Because Gates are hostile, high-mana environments, simply casting magic inside them is more taxing. Every spell cast incurs a flat Threshold penalty based on the Gate's rank:

  • E-Rank Gate: +0 T
  • D-Rank Gate: +1 T per spell
  • C-Rank Gate: +2 T per spell
  • B-Rank Gate: +3 T per spell
  • A-Rank Gate: +5 T per spell
  1. Failure Still Costs
  • If a spell fails, the failure is localized to the spell being cast (it does not cascade to other maintained spells).
  • However, the caster still takes a minimum 1-point Threshold cost for the effort. Magic is tiring even when it misfires.
  • Casting a new spell while maintaining an active spell imposes a –2 penalty to the roll per maintained spell, increasing the chance of this failure.
  1. Calamities (Backlash)

If a mage's accumulated Threshold exceeds their Safe Limit, they must roll on the Calamity table. Backlash scales by how far over the limit they pushed:

  • Minor: Nosebleeds, severe fatigue, minor spell warping.
  • Moderate: Physical injury, severe backlash, environmental damage.
  • Severe: Unintended summoning, physical mutation, catastrophic Gate reaction (e.g., accelerating a Dungeon Break).
  1. The Golden Rule: Use Spent MoS, not Rolled MoS

If you base the Threshold on the raw MoS the player rolls, you accidentally punish high-skill mages. If Lena rolls a critical success and gets an MoS of 10, but only wanted to light a campfire, it shouldn't cost her massive Threshold.

Instead, the rule must be: Threshold Cost = Total SPENT MoS ÷ 2 (Rounded Up).

You only pay for the power you actually inject into the spell.

  1. How the Math Looks in Play

Because GURPS normally rounds point costs up, dividing by two creates a beautifully smooth, predictable curve:

  • Spend 1–2 MoS (Basic effect) = 1 T
  • Spend 3–4 MoS (Solid combat spell) = 2 T
  • Spend 5–6 MoS (Advanced AOE/Damage) = 3 T
  • Spend 7–8 MoS (Elite power) = 4 T
  • Spend 9–10 MoS (S-Rank devastation) = 5 T
  1. The Consequence: Magic Just Got More Efficient

In your old system, a "Major/Ultimate" spell cost 7–10 T. Under your new MoS / 2 rule, a massive 10 MoS spell only costs 5 T.

This means mages like Lena and Tessa will be able to cast about twice as many spells before risking a Calamity backlash. For a Solo Leveling inspired dungeon-crawl campaign, this is actually a good thing. It gives them the stamina to survive a multi-room C-Rank Gate without needing to take a nap after two fights.

  1. How It Connects to Your Existing Rules

This new math slots perfectly into the safety governors you've already built:

  • The Magery Soft Cap: You already ruled that spending more MoS in a single category (like Damage) than your Magery level doubles the Threshold cost for the over-cap portion. This stays perfectly intact. (e.g., If Tessa has Magery 3 and spends 5 MoS on Damage, the first 3 MoS are cheap, but the last 2 MoS cost double).
  • Gate Penalties: If they are in a C-Rank Gate (which imposes a +2 T penalty per spell), a 4-MoS fireball costs 2 T (Base) + 2 T (Gate Penalty) = 4 T Total.
  • Failure Cost: You previously established that failing a spell costs a flat 1 T. Under this new math, 1 T is exactly the cost of a minimum-effort spell. This makes logical sense—you burn the equivalent of a basic cantrip just trying to wrestle the mana.

Final Verdict

Change your rule document to read:

"A spell generates Threshold equal to half of the total MoS spent on its effects (rounded up). MoS rolled but not spent generates no Threshold."
